A nationwide directory of research opportunities with universities and companies. Built first for community college students, who usually don't have a research office doing the legwork for them — but useful for any undergrad, at a CC or a four-year, looking for a way in.

Application Help

the part a research office would normally do for you

Cold Email to a PI

the single biggest unlock if you don't have a listed opening to apply to 
Subject: Undergraduate research interest — [Your Name], [Your College]

Dear Dr. [Last Name],

I'm a [year] student at [Community College], studying [major/focus]. I came across your work on [one specific thing from their lab page or a recent paper — be specific, this is what shows you actually looked], and I'm reaching out to ask whether you have any openings for an undergraduate researcher, paid or volunteer, for [semester/summer].

A bit about me: [one or two lines — a relevant project, class, or hands-on skill. Concrete beats impressive: "built X" beats "passionate about science"].

I understand community college students aren't always the first group labs think to include, but I'd welcome the chance to contribute and learn. Would you be open to a brief 15-minute call to discuss? I've attached my resume/transcript, and I'm happy to send anything else that would help.

Thank you for your time,
[Your Name]
[Phone / email]

Why this works: a specific ask (a short call) gets answered more than an open-ended "let me know." In a large lab, emailing a grad student or postdoc first often gets a faster, more honest response than going straight to the PI. No response after 1–2 weeks is normal — one polite follow-up is expected, more than that isn't.

Research Interest Statement

1-page structure most REU/CCOP-style applications ask for 
1. OPENING (2-3 sentences)
What pulled you toward this field. Be specific and personal — a class, a project, a problem you noticed — not a generic "I've always loved science" or a childhood origin story.

2. WHAT YOU'VE ALREADY DONE (1 paragraph)
Coursework, projects, hackathons, jobs — anything that shows initiative or hands-on ability, even outside a lab. Frame informal experience in research-relevant terms (e.g. troubleshooting, iteration, working with data).

3. WHY THIS PROGRAM SPECIFICALLY (1 paragraph)
Name the actual lab/focus area. Vague interest reads as a form letter; specific interest reads as someone who did the homework.

4. ACCESS & CONTEXT, IF RELEVANT (2-3 sentences)
If you're first-gen, low-income, or your school has limited research opportunities, say so directly. Many CC-friendly programs are funded specifically to reach students like you — this isn't oversharing, it's relevant.

5. WHAT YOU WANT TO GET OUT OF IT (2-3 sentences)
Tie it to a real next step — transfer major, grad school, a career direction — not just "an opportunity."

6. CLOSING (1-2 sentences)
Reaffirm interest, thank them for considering you.

Why this works: reviewers can tell a form letter instantly — tailor section 3 for every program you send this to. Avoid self-labeling like "I'm the ideal candidate;" let the specifics in sections 2 and 4 make that case instead.
